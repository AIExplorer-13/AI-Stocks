MACROECONOMIC FOUNDATION

[CONCEPT]
Macroeconomics studies the overall performance of an economy using aggregate variables like GDP, inflation, and interest rates.

[INSIGHT]
Macroeconomic variables are essential for modeling risk and predicting asset returns.

[CONCEPT]
The circular flow model states that total spending in an economy equals total income.

[INSIGHT]
This ensures equilibrium between production and consumption.

🔵 GDP (GROSS DOMESTIC PRODUCT)

[CONCEPT]
GDP represents the total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country.

[FORMULA]
GDP (Y) = C + I + G + (X − M)

[INSIGHT]
GDP measures economic output using consumption, investment, government spending, and net exports.

[TABLE_ROW]
Component: Consumption (C)
Driver: Disposable Income
Sensitivity: High

[TABLE_ROW]
Component: Investment (I)
Driver: Interest Rates
Sensitivity: Very High

[TABLE_ROW]
Component: Government Spending (G)
Driver: Fiscal Policy
Sensitivity: Low

[TABLE_ROW]
Component: Net Exports (X−M)
Driver: Exchange Rates
Sensitivity: High

[RULE]
If interest rates increase → Then investment (I) decreases

[RULE]
If disposable income increases → Then consumption (C) increases

🟡 INFLATION

[CONCEPT]
Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level, reducing purchasing power.

[INSIGHT]
Inflation reduces real returns and influences central bank policy decisions.

[CONCEPT]
Demand-pull inflation occurs when demand exceeds supply.

[CONCEPT]
Cost-push inflation occurs due to rising production costs.

[RULE]
If demand exceeds supply → Then prices increase (demand-pull inflation)

[RULE]
If production costs rise → Then prices increase (cost-push inflation)

🟠 QUANTITY THEORY OF MONEY

[FORMULA]
M × V = P × Y

[INSIGHT]
Money supply and velocity determine price levels and economic output.

[RULE]
If money supply (M) increases without output growth → Then inflation increases

🔴 INTEREST RATES & POLICY

[CONCEPT]
Interest rates determine the discount rate for future cash flows.

[FORMULA]
i = r* + π + 0.5(π − π*) + 0.5(y − ȳ)

[INSIGHT]
The Taylor Rule guides central banks in setting interest rates based on inflation and output gap.

[RULE]
If inflation rises above target → Then central bank increases interest rates

[RULE]
If economic output is below potential → Then central bank lowers interest rates

[INSIGHT]
Central banks must increase nominal rates more than inflation to control real rates (Taylor Principle).

Macroeconomic variables define constraints for financial markets.

[INSIGHT]
