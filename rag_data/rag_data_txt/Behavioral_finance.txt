[CONCEPT]
Behavioral Finance studies how psychology influences financial market behavior.

[CONCEPT]
Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) assumes markets are rational and prices reflect all available information.

[INSIGHT]
Behavioral finance explains market inefficiencies caused by human emotions and biases.

[CONCEPT]
Markets are driven by fear and greed rather than purely rational decisions.

[INSIGHT]
Emotional behavior creates predictable patterns like bubbles and crashes.

[RULE]
If market sentiment is driven by extreme greed → Then asset prices may be overvalued and prone to correction

[RULE]
If market sentiment is driven by extreme fear → Then assets may be undervalued and present buying opportunities

[CONCEPT]
Herd behavior occurs when investors follow the crowd instead of independent analysis.

[INSIGHT]
Herd behavior leads to asset bubbles and sharp market crashes.

[RULE]
If investors exhibit herd behavior → Then market volatility and mispricing increase

[CONCEPT]
Recency bias causes investors to overweight recent trends while ignoring long-term fundamentals.

[INSIGHT]
This leads to momentum chasing and poor long-term decisions.

[RULE]
If recent market performance is strong → Then investors may overestimate future returns due to recency bias

[CONCEPT]
Overconfidence bias leads investors to overestimate their knowledge and predictive ability.

[INSIGHT]
This results in excessive trading and lower returns.

[RULE]
If trading frequency is high → Then overconfidence bias is likely present

[CONCEPT]
Loss aversion means losses are felt more strongly than gains.

[INSIGHT]
Investors tend to hold losing assets too long and sell winning assets too early.

[RULE]
If an investor holds losing positions for too long → Then loss aversion bias is influencing decisions

[CONCEPT]
Anchoring bias occurs when investors rely too heavily on past prices or initial information.

[INSIGHT]
This prevents proper adjustment to new market conditions.

[RULE]
If investment decisions are based on past prices → Then anchoring bias may distort judgment

[CONCEPT]
Market crashes follow a behavioral cycle: boom → euphoria → trigger → panic.

[INSIGHT]
Emotional contagion causes rapid selling and liquidity collapse during crashes.

[RULE]
If panic selling increases → Then market liquidity decreases and volatility rises
